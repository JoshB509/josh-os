1. REPAIR THE CLOCK, NOT JUST THE ERROR MESSAGE
Installed media.py::probe prefers average_rate over guessed_rate. This records
14882500/496083 (about 30.00002016 fps), which export.py rejects because it is
neither an integer nor a supported n*1000/1001 rate.
Existing probe evidence: base_rate=30, guessed_rate=30, time_base=1/90000;
29,765 video packets; 19,842 PTS deltas of 2,970 ticks (33ms), 9,921 of 3,060
ticks (34ms), and one of 9,000 ticks (100ms). Last PTS was 89,298,000 ticks
(992.2s), while reported container duration was 992.166s.
This points to a nominal 30fps clock with millisecond timestamp rounding and an
end gap/bounds inconsistency, not demonstrated sustained variable-rate capture.

Required implementation: preserve measured PTS and reported average separately
from the chosen sequence/media clock; validate all PTS against the nominal grid,
reject duplicate/reversed timestamps, record gaps, and establish safe video AND
audio bounds including the end discrepancy. Use 30fps only after that check.
Do not round arbitrary files to 30, silently remove the exporter guard, or merely
replace the fps field in the old timing map/XML. Truly irregular VFR needs its
own verified original-referencing mapping, or an explicit early failure.

Rebuild one authoritative source-to-output map from the same source-second cut
decisions/transcript/acoustic evidence on the validated clock. Keep audio seconds
and cut padding protected. Test first/last frames, the end gap, cut joins, cue
mapping and cumulative drift. Premiere must agree on source-time interpretation.
