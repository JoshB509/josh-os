# Curiosity lines · Executive Communication Program

A bank of lines for checkout pages, the sales page, emails, ads, carousels and lead magnet pages, written the way Parker writes his "inside you'll discover" bullets. Each one names a pain the whole audience feels, offers a tangible thing that fixes it without explaining how, and ends on what happens because of it. Built from `audience-communication.md` and the lesson map.

Created 6 Oct 2026. Rewritten the same day after Josh's notes on the first version.

## How they're built

1. **Name the core pain or outcome, broad enough that anyone in any job reads it and thinks "that's me".** Executives not getting it, good ideas shot down, meetings that go nowhere, work that sounds smaller than it is, decks that take a weekend. If a line only fits one scene, go up a level and find what sits underneath it.
2. **Give them a tangible thing, never how it works.** A phrase, a question, the 4 slides, the 2-step order, the one page. Something they could picture themselves doing, with the result it gets. "The one phrase you can use to get any executive to back your idea" works. "The phrase that gives executives a choice instead of a yes or no" fails, because it explains the method and makes the mechanism the prize. Lines with no tangible thing at all ("how to get your ideas approved") go stale, so most lines carry one. Counts match what's in the lessons, and big ones stay out because they read as homework. Names of ideas taught inside the course (momentum, the skimmer and the reader, the black box) stay out too, along with anything a reader would only understand after the lesson.
3. **End on what happens because of it.** The yes, the approval, the decision on the spot, the evening back. Being first, putting something first or leading with something is a step, and nobody buys a step.
4. **Add a bracket that answers the objection** when one fits. The bank is at the bottom.
5. **Keep the register.** No hype, no identity promises (presence, gravitas, confidence), no "unlock", "transform" or "master". Masterclass lines stay off promotion stakes.

There is no swipe file in the masterclass, so no line mentions one.

Use four to eight at a time on a page, picked from different sections.

## Headline pattern

Parker's checkout headline is the outcome, then the objection in brackets. The same pattern in this voice:

- Get a decision in the meeting, even when they asked for more detail last time
- Get your board paper approved with nobody there to present it
- Know exactly what to say when an executive pushes back, without getting louder or playing politics
- Build the deck in an hour and walk in knowing it gets a yes (even if you've got forty slides)
