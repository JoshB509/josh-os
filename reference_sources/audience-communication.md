# Communication audience · pains, words and outcomes

The reference file for anything written about Heard at the Top. Sales page, emails, lesson hooks, ads, the course map. Same job as `audience.md` does for the programme, with the communication buyer split in two.

Sources: strategy calls with Abhishek (18 Sep, senior, Citi director laid off, negotiating a new role), Jessica (11 Sep, leads a team of seven, wants director), Saad (4 Sep, principal PM, wants director), Daniel (9 Sep, consultant gone permanent at a bank), Nathalie (16 Sep, project manager, told her manager will not back her). Plus the programme and communication sales pages, and `audience.md`. Quotes are verbatim from the transcripts, cleaned only of ums.

Two rules for using it.

**Write to the frustration, quote the person.** The verbatim lines are evidence of what the frustration sounds like when one person says it. Copy is written one level up, at the frustration itself, so it reaches everyone who has it. Nathalie was asked for detail, told it was too much, then called defensive. The frustration under that is being made to explain what you've already explained, and being made to feel you didn't have the whole picture when you did all the work. The first is her story. The second is the line. Test for any line: would three people in three different jobs read it and think "that's me"? If it only fits the person who said it, go up a level.

**Moments, not mechanisms.** A line that describes a mechanism gets rewritten until it describes a moment in their week. "The report was automated" becomes "you walk into the Monday meeting already knowing every number they're about to ask for."

---

## The one problem underneath both buyers

They know more than the people they're trying to convince, and it never counts.

They give senior people facts, and what comes back is "can you add more detail", or "let's park this", or "thanks, that's useful", or a question, then another, then a third, until they're defending instead of explaining. And afterwards nothing moves. The decision doesn't get made, the credit goes to whoever presented the summary, and the picture leadership holds of them is "helpful", which is the picture that gets you thanked and never gets you moved.

Both buyers describe this. The senior one describes it in bigger numbers and the stuck one describes it in more pain. It's the same thing.

**The frame that makes it click, in their words.** Senior people don't take in information the way you give it. They listen for a decision, a risk, and what it means for the thing they're accountable for. Everything else is noise to them however much it matters to you. So when you explain, they hear detail, and when you cut the detail, they hear vagueness, because neither version was built for the way they listen. Which is why it feels like a different language. It is.

# A · The senior buyer

## Who they are

Director, head of, principal, senior manager with a team. Ten to twenty years in. Delivery has never been the question. They have a specific piece of communication they give often or have to get right, the steering committee update, the board paper, the quarterly review, the pitch for headcount, and they know it isn't doing what it should.

They can name the gap and they can't fix it. Jessica's version: "I fundamentally feel like I'm missing executive presence and I don't know, like what, what the criteria that is." Saad's version: "I look at my transcripts after a call and I notice that my communication could be more crisp." Abhishek's: he loses ground when leadership puts him under pressure, gets marched through questions, and watches other people present his work more simply and take the credit.

They are one or two levels below where they want to be, and they have worked out that the next level is decided on how they come across rather than on what they deliver. They just don't know what "come across" is made of.

## The core frustrations

What the quotes below point at, one level up. Copy is written from this list. The quote is where it came from.

- **Doing the senior job and being heard as the level below.** They carry the responsibility and the decisions, and when they speak, senior people hear a capable manager rather than a peer. *(Jessica: "it's giving more junior")*
- **Told what's missing, never what it's made of.** They've been given a word for the gap, presence, gravitas, crispness, and nobody can tell them what to do on Thursday. *(Jessica: "I don't know what the criteria is")*
- **Their work sounds smaller than it is when they describe it.** They can hear the better version the moment someone else says it, and they can't produce it themselves. *(Jessica: "the way that you frame it, it sounds so much better")*
- **Heard as the specialist rather than the person in charge.** The expertise that got them here is the thing that now gets in the way. *(Saad: "I come off more as an engineer")*
- **Losing ground under pressure from above.** Questions from senior people turn into a march, and they come out of it having defended rather than led. *(Abhishek)*
- **Someone with a simpler version of their work gets the credit for it.** The value of what they did is decided by whoever describes it best, and that is often not them. *(Abhishek, Saad: "the glory goes to the team that produced the model")*
- **The moments that decide things arrive without warning.** The question at the end of the one-to-one, the ask from left field, and no way to handle it in the moment. *(Saad: "that usually catches me off")*
- **Getting there costs too much.** They reach the clear version every time, on their own, and it takes a weekend they don't have. *(Saad: "it takes a lot of thinking through on my own")*
- **Delivery has stopped being what moves them.** Everything that got them here has stopped working, and they were promised the next cycle, again. *(Jessica: "top of the list", Daniel: "I've had to shift my perspective on delivery")*
- **Wanting to be handed the line.** Not the theory, the words. *(Jessica: "I wish I had an earpiece")*

## What they'll be able to do

Each of these is written in their world. The mechanism is in brackets for reference and never goes on the page.

- Walk into the review and say in three sentences what happened, what it means, and what you want them to decide, and watch the meeting go where you pointed it. *(BENS, position before detail, the running order)*

- Be the person the senior team turns to when the decision is on the table, rather than the person they ask for the numbers behind it. *(momentum, the driver)*
- Walk into a meeting you couldn't prepare for and drive it anyway, because the moves are the same whichever way it goes. *(Part 6 as a whole)*
- Get to the clear version of your message in twenty minutes instead of a weekend, every time, using the same four tests. *(outcomes not outputs)*

## Objections and beliefs to write against

- "I've done presentation training." Training teaches delivery. This is what a senior audience takes in and acts on, which is why people come back from training standing better and still being asked for a shorter version.
- "My executives are unusually hard to read." Senior people listen in the same shape everywhere, for the decision, the risk and what it means for the thing they own.
- "I don't have time." Most of this happens inside the meetings and papers already in your week. You do the same things differently.
- "It's politics." None of this asks you to talk over anyone or take credit. It's your work and your view arriving in a shape the people who decide can act on.
- "I already know this." They know the principle. What they don't have is the line for Thursday.

# B · The stuck buyer

## Who they are

Manager or senior individual contributor, respected for the work, one or two levels below where they want to be. They say they want to "communicate better" and when you ask what that means they describe symptoms rather than a skill. They get sent back for more detail. They get talked in circles. They sound defensive when they're only answering the question. They can feel there's a different language at the top and they can't hear what it is.

Nathalie is the reference. Project manager in a multinational, French first language, recognised for the work, and her line manager has told her outright he won't back her for promotion. Her own diagnosis is communication. Her exact frame for it: "how I think about things and then when I start talking, it's like this completely disappears."

Their frustration is higher than the senior buyer's because they have no name for the problem and no evidence it can be fixed. They have often been given the feedback, "be more concise", "you come across as defensive", with nothing attached to it. Several have concluded it's a personality thing, or a language thing, or a them thing.

## The core frustrations

What Nathalie's lines point at, one level up. This is the list to write from, because the stuck buyer in a bank, a hospital or a software company has the same list and none of her sentences.

- **Being made to explain what you've already explained.** The same thing, again, to the same people, and it still doesn't seem to go in. *(the detail loop)*
- **Being made to feel you didn't have the whole picture, when you did all the work.** They had everything they needed to decide and behaved as if they didn't. *("they already have all the info I can bring")*
- **Whatever amount you give is the wrong amount.** Too much, then not enough, and nobody can tell you which they want. *("you asked me for detail")*
- **Answering the question reads as arguing.** You're only replying to what they asked and somehow you're the defensive one. *("you're being defensive")*
- **Conversations that go nowhere.** You go in knowing what you want and come out having given up on it, and things that should be simple take three meetings. *("okay, whatever")*
- **Knowing what you think and not being able to say it the way you think it.** The gap between how clear it is in your head and how it comes out. *("it completely disappears")*
- **Suspecting executives speak another language, and not knowing where you learn it.** The people who get heard seem to know something you don't, and nobody will tell you what. *("technical, technical, technical")*
- **Doing the work and watching someone else get thanked for it.** The credit goes to whoever said it best. *("they took my work")*
- **Feedback you can't do anything with.** Be more concise. Be less defensive. Maybe some training. Nothing you can act on by Thursday. *("no path forward")*
- **Doing more has stopped working and you don't know what replaces it.** Volume got you respected and not moved. *("I thought it was by being the person who does volume")*
- **Wondering if it's you.** The language, the personality, the accent, or just you.

- "My boss won't back me anyway." Understood, and the people above your boss are listening too. This is how they hear you.
- "I've been told this before." Told what. "Be more concise" is a symptom, not an instruction. This is the instruction.
- "I don't have time." The escalation meeting is happening anyway. This changes what you say in it.

**In-their-world rewrites, examples of the rule**

- The report was automated → you walk into the Monday meeting already knowing every number they're about to ask for.
- We saved two days a month → the regional managers got their Sunday evenings back, and the Monday call stopped being about whose numbers were right.
- Fifteen per cent throughput → the backlog that had been a standing item at the board was cleared before budget season, and nobody had to make the headcount case.
- I learned a framework for pushback → the senior person who's already decided can't talk you round any more, because you've got the line for that.
- Better presentations → the director reads your titles top to bottom and has the argument before you've spoken.

## Lines to avoid

- Anything about mechanism where an outcome would go. "Learn the BENS framework" tells them nothing. "Build the case for anything in ten minutes and know it'll hold" tells them what they get.
- Identity promises. Presence, gravitas, inspire, become a leader. This audience distrusts them and the course doesn't deliver them.
- Guarantees of promotion or results. They're senior enough to smell it.
- "Communicate with confidence." Confidence is what they get after the line works. Sell the line.
- Voice, delivery, body language. Say plainly that it isn't in here.
- Hustle framing, urgency theatre, "unlock", "transform", "master".
- Telling them why it matters. Say the thing that matters.

## Note on the two buyers in one course

They don't need separate paths. Part 1 opens on both sets of symptoms because they're the same cause, and the senior buyer reads the stuck buyer's lines and thinks "that was me five years ago and it's still me on a bad day". What changes is which lines go at the top of the page. The stuck buyer's pain is louder and more searchable, so it leads the sales copy. The senior buyer's outcomes carry more money, so they lead the outcomes section.
