PASS 1 - SYNCHRONIZED PRESENTER MASTER
1. Fingerprint original video and supplied clean microphone audio. Validate
   source bounds, cadence/PTS, audio sample rate and supported export clock.
2. Measure the clean mic's alignment to the actual video/reference. Check drift
   at multiple points and report confidence and residuals; never reuse a fixed
   offset from another composite. Uncertain sync blocks approval/export.
3. Key the foreground with CorridorKey. Apply adjustable garbage mask, safe
   table crop, presenter framing, background, shadow and subtle shifting light.
   Preserve raw alpha/master and settings; check hands, hair and gesture envelope.
   Spatial treatment must not change source timing. Keep original recovery media.
4. Produce accurate word timestamps against this synchronized, uncut master.
   Retain original audio/video coordinates and alignment provenance. Validate
   monotonicity, bounds, low-confidence words and sampled word/waveform alignment.
5. Present a reviewable Pass 1 result, sync evidence and exceptions. Josh approves
   this exact revision before Pass 2. Record actor, time and input/output hashes.
   Earlier keying-fixture approval must not auto-approve this pass.

PASS 2 - BUILD THE CLEAN CUT
1. Remove unwanted silence and tighten pacing while preserving intelligibility,
   consonant attacks, word tails, meaningful pauses and natural breathing.
2. Refine joins using waveform evidence. Use tiny AUDIO crossfades only where
   suitable and supported by safe handles. Do not apply indiscriminate video
   dissolves. Keep the chosen timeline duration and audio/video sync unchanged.
   Crossfade duration/curve/bounds must be explicit and reviewable, not a magic
   global preset. Insufficient handles or overlapping speech require a different
   boundary or a flagged join; never reintroduce rejected speech through handles.
3. Transcribe the actual tightened cut, with source/output coordinate mapping.
   Give that transcript, audio and join context to the assistant for a semantic
   false-start sweep: incomplete/rephrased attempts, stumbles and duplicate takes.
   Preserve intentional repetition, useful improvisation and meaningful emphasis.
4. Record each rejected range with reason, evidence and coordinate-space/version.
   Apply the removals to the retained ranges and exported timeline. A flag or
   exception row alone is not a removal. Ambiguous cases remain visible for review.
5. Rebuild the exact kept timeline and validate final words. Remap retained words
   only when fully contained in validated spans; retranscribe/re-align boundary
   words and changed joins, or the full cut if needed. Do not keep stale timestamps
   or pretend a word spanning a removal remains whole. Resolve uncertain words.
6. Perform listening and join QA: every cut, clean speech onset/tail, no clicks,
   unintended echoes/double words, excessive dead air, clipped speech or sync drift.
   Record automated checks, human listening and unresolved issues separately.
7. Present the clean cut, transcript, map and QA to Josh. Approval freezes this
   exact revision as the authority for all subsequent visual design.

APPROVED CLEAN CUT CONTRACT
The exact kept timeline is authoritative, not the script, an earlier transcript,
an animation's duration or reconstructed automatic edits. Store immutable clean
cut ID/revision, source fingerprints, rational frame/PTS and audio-sample mapping,
ordered retained spans, cut/join decisions, validated word timing, QA and approval.
The output duration and retained sequence must agree across audio, video and XML.
Audio transitions include a separate handle/envelope map without silently changing
the approved output clock or adding rejected speech. Disclose timing precision.

Any subsequent edit of words, kept ranges, sync, speed or duration creates a NEW
clean-cut revision, invalidates dependent approvals/shot mappings and requires
explicit review. Visual changes alone cannot silently trim or extend the clean
cut. An approved manually polished timeline must be imported and reconciled to
the same contract; never regenerate it by rerunning the original auto-cut plan.

AFTER CLEAN-CUT APPROVAL - SHOT LIST AND HTML
Design shots from the approved final transcript and timeline. Each graphic has
an explicit purpose, entry, end, state and exit back to presenter (or a specified
next shot). Use sufficient reading time, then return to Josh; do not sit on an
animation waiting for the next beat. Frozen asset handles are editing capacity,
not an instruction to hold them on the timeline indefinitely.

For multi-part explanations, create a recurring checkpoint graphic: initial
overview, completed parts, current part and next part. Return to it at section
transitions to orient the viewer and reset attention. Preserve visual identity
and cumulative state across returns; do not replay the whole introduction.
Make section/step labels come from the actual content, not invented structures.

Render shots independently with declared incoming state, scene isolation,
motion duration, hold and exit. Preserve phase across split clips where needed.
Validate no outgoing/incoming scene leakage. Keep graphics independently editable
with source handles and reversible presenter layouts. Export editable XML via
the existing supported route; do not fabricate native Premiere project files.
